# Removing information about yourself

## Deleting your own accounts

**Before deleting, find what to delete.** A single username usually turns up more accounts
than you remember yourself: old forums, services you signed up for once, sites that
registered you automatically through your email. For a quick self-check there is the
[Maigret bot](https://maigret.app/co-en) — it looks up accounts by username across thousands
of sites, with nothing to install.

Despite the fact that the Internet remembers everything, deleting accounts remains one of the strongest means against basic intelligence on you.
It seems that this is a trivial topic, but there are several important nuances:

Before deleting:
1. Request the service to **export your personal data** under GDPR. This will help in the next steps and give you an understanding of what
the service knows about you.
2. **Rename your account**, delete/change photos, wait a few days. This is necessary so that indexer sites and search engines
have time to update the information about your account, and subsequently you cannot be found by its latest copy. Also, do not forget that
from now on you cannot leave any comments and messages publicly.
3. All old comments/tweets/messages **should preferably be deleted**. There are mechanisms in social networks and messengers that allow others to reach you even
after deletion: mentions (a link to your profile that looks like a name), authorship marks, reposts/retweets.
It's worth getting rid of all such leads.
4. Update the search results by asking them to reindex your **modified page** ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/advanced/crawling/ask-google-to-recrawl?hl=ru)) and wait for a while so that other systems that save information from social networks will also receive a new version of your account.

After that, the account can be deleted using the platform's built-in tools.

### Deletion request

Not all services allow deletion through regular means. Or it is hidden so deep that it is impossible to find.
Fortunately, there are auxiliary sites with information on how to delete your account.

- [JustDeleteMe](https://justdeleteme.xyz/) - an excellent directory for platforms, gives links and a description of the necessary actions,
shows the complexity of removal. The creators also have related sites [JustGetMyData](https://justgetmydata.com/) and
[JustWhatsTheData](https://justwhatsthedata.github.io/) to fulfill paragraph 1 above.

- [AccountKiller](https://www.accountkiller.com/en/all-sites) - removal instructions for a large number of sites, mainly Western.

Even if you couldn't find the removal instructions for the site you need, don't despair. There is always the option to write
to support and request the deletion of your account under GDPR.

## Deleting search results in search engines

Google offers a number of different forms through which you can request the removal and updating of content in search results.

- [Removing outdated content from search](https://search.google.com/search-console/remove-outdated-content) - well suited for pages that need to be reindexed (see above point 4 about deleting accounts).

- [Google Personal Data Deletion Request Form](https://reportcontent.google.com/forms/rtbf?hl=ru)

- [New request form for deletion of personal data](https://support.google.com/websearch/troubleshooter/9685456)

## Removing information about leaks

[Brief instructions for deleting your data from popular services](https://inteltechniques.com/exposure.html) - Have I Been Pwned, Leakcheck, IntelX, etc.

To make requests to services that are interested in storing your data, you need to prepare a complete list of all the information that you want to clean up. It should include:
- nicknames
- phones
- emails
- addresses
- car license plates
- government identifiers (passport number, social security number, taxpayer identification number, full name, etc.)
- any data from leaked database records
   
### Removing yourself from people-search bots

Earlier this guide contained step-by-step instructions for deleting your data from
specific Telegram bots. Almost none of those bots exist any more.

**On 9 December 2024 article 272.1 of the Russian Criminal Code came into force** — it
criminalises the unlawful collection, storage, use and transfer of personal data, and,
separately, the creation and maintenance of resources that distribute it. The black market
for people-search services shrank noticeably after that, and Telegram began deleting such
bots.

In late February 2025 Eye of God, the best known service of this kind, suspended its work:
its creator explained that he was waiting for the legal situation to become clear.

### How to remove yourself where it is still possible

If you have found out that someone is searching for data on you, try to obtain the bot's
message itself or its report: the report leads you both to the service's website and to the
Telegram bot. Then look inside for the option to delete your own data — it hides in a
section like "Account" or "Profile", and sometimes it is not there at all and the only way
left is to write to support.

And the main thing — **removal can cost money, and any amount of it**. One of the large
services charges hundreds of dollars for each type of data separately: full name with date
of birth, phone number, email. The wording it uses to explain this is worth reading in full:

> The service is expensive, because hiding data is an exception to the principle of free
> access to information. When privacy is a luxury.
>
> We are obliged to provide the option — and we have provided it.
> We are not obliged to make it affordable — and we have not.

In other words, the removal mechanism exists in order to fulfil an obligation, and the price
is set so that nobody uses it.

It happens more gently, but still not for free: another bot lets you hide only the country
your account is registered in, and only with a paid subscription that also includes bulk
searches and higher limits.

### What to do if you are not ready to pay

The legal lever appeared together with that same article 272.1: the activity of such services
falls directly under criminal liability, and you can demand that the processing stop and the
data be deleted by filing a complaint with Roskomnadzor — through its electronic reception
or the public services portal.

But the lever has an unpleasant property, and it is better to know about it in advance: **the
complaint has to name the operator** — who exactly processes your data. And the surviving
services are anonymous, so there is nothing to name.

So the realistic order is this: remove yourself where the procedure exists and is free — from
leak-checking services and from operators like GetContact below. For the rest, assume the data
has already spread, and work with the [original source](./breaches.md) — the leaks these bots
are built from.

### Disabling search for your number in GetContact

In GetContact, it is possible to disable the display of names known by your number at will, or disable the search entirely.

To do this, follow [this link](https://www.getcontact.com/en/manage/profile) and log in via messenger, then turn it off.

It should also be noted that in this section of the site you can track search queries by your number. If you disable the search, this feature is lost,
as well as receiving notifications about searches for you from the app. However, when you log in to the GetContact application again, this feature is restored.
